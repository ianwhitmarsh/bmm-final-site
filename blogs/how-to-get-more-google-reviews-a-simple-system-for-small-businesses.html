How to Get More Google Reviews: A Simple System for Small Businesses

Big Mammoth MediaOct 7, 2026 · Digital Marketing Education
Most happy customers are glad to leave a review. They just don't think to do it on their own. A simple system for asking turns the good work you're already doing into proof new customers can see.

Why Reviews Matter

Before most people call a local business, they check its reviews. A steady flow of recent, positive reviews tells them you're trustworthy and active, and it's one of the factors Google considers when deciding which businesses to show in local results. The businesses with the most reviews usually aren't the ones with the happiest customers; they're the ones that ask.

Step 1: Get Your Direct Review Link

In your Google Business Profile, look for the option to ask for reviews. Google gives you a short link that opens the review box directly. Save it somewhere you can paste it quickly: your phone, your email signature, your invoicing software and your CRM.

Step 2: Ask at the Right Moment

The best time to ask is right after a win: the job is finished and the customer is happy, they've just thanked you, or they've seen results. Asking in person first and then following up with the link works far better than a cold email weeks later.

A simple way to say it: "I'm really glad this worked out. Reviews help small businesses like ours a lot. Would you mind leaving us a quick one on Google? I'll text you the link."

Step 3: Make It Ridiculously Easy

  • Text the link right after you ask. Most people leave reviews from their phone.
  • Keep the message short: thank them, include the link, and say it only takes a minute.
  • Add a QR code to receipts, business cards, invoices and the counter at your front desk.
  • Add the link to your email signature and your thank-you emails.

Step 4: Follow Up Once

People mean to do it and forget. A single friendly reminder a few days later is fine. More than that starts to feel pushy, so stop after one.

Step 5: Make It a Habit for the Whole Team

Reviews work best as part of your process, not a one-off push. Decide who asks and when (for example, the technician at the end of every job), and track how many reviews come in each month. A simple goal, like two or three new reviews a week, keeps it front of mind. If your CRM can send the review request automatically when a job is marked complete, use it.

Step 6: Reply to Every Review

Responding shows future customers that you care and that real people run the business.

  • Positive reviews: thank them by name and mention something specific about the job.
  • Negative reviews: stay calm, thank them for the feedback, apologize for their experience, and offer to make it right offline with a phone number or email. Don't argue in public. A thoughtful reply to a bad review can earn more trust than a dozen five-star ones.

What Not to Do

  • Don't offer discounts, gifts or payment for reviews. It breaks Google's rules and can get reviews removed.
  • Don't only ask customers you think will leave five stars. Google's policies don't allow selectively asking for positive reviews, and an honest mix looks more believable anyway.
  • Don't write reviews yourself or ask friends and family to pose as customers. Fake reviews can lead to penalties and they erode trust when people notice.
  • Don't ask everyone at once. A sudden flood of reviews followed by silence looks odd. Steady beats spiky.

Put Your Reviews to Work

Once you have great reviews, use them. Feature your favorites on your website, share them on social media, and add a line like "Rated 5 stars by our customers" to your ads and proposals. Reviews are some of the most persuasive marketing you have, and your customers wrote them for you.

Final Thoughts

Getting more reviews isn't about tricks. It's about doing good work, asking every time, and making it effortless to say yes. Build those three habits and your reviews will keep growing month after month.
